# Startup Policies & Best Practices
Source: Startup India, DPIIT Policy Documents
Last Verified: 2024

## National Startup Policy Framework

The Government of India launched the Startup India Action Plan in 2016 with three pillars:
1. **Simplification and Handholding**
2. **Funding Support and Incentives**
3. **Industry-Academia Partnership and Incubation**

## Key Definitions

### What is a Startup? (DPIIT Definition)
An entity is recognised as a startup if:
- Incorporated as Private Ltd Company, Registered Partnership Firm, or LLP
- Up to 10 years from date of incorporation
- Annual turnover not exceeding INR 100 crore in any financial year
- Working towards innovation/development/improvement of products, processes, services
- Has potential to generate employment or create wealth

## State Startup Policies

Most Indian states have their own startup policies:

### Karnataka Startup Policy
- Karnataka leads India's startup ecosystem (Bengaluru = Silicon Valley of India)
- Programs: Karnataka Startup Cell, ELEVATE (INR 50 lakh equity-free grants)
- Website: https://startupkarnataka.gov.in

### Maharashtra Startup Policy
- Mumbai/Pune key hubs
- MahaStartup scheme: Grants and incubation support
- Website: https://udyog.mahaonline.gov.in

### Gujarat Startup Policy
- iCreate incubator support
- Student Startup and Innovation Policy (SSIP)
- Website: https://ic-startup.gujarat.gov.in

### Telangana Startup Policy
- T-Hub: World's largest startup incubator
- WE Hub: Women entrepreneur support
- Website: https://t-hub.co

### Tamil Nadu Startup Policy
- StartupTN: State startup promotion body
- TANSIM: Investment promotion
- Website: https://startuptn.in

### Delhi Startup Policy
- Dedicated startup fund
- NSUT incubation support

## Women Entrepreneur Schemes

### Mahila Udyam Nidhi Scheme (SIDBI)
- Soft loans for women entrepreneurs
- Up to INR 10 lakh

### TREAD (Trade Related Entrepreneurship Assistance and Development)
- Ministry of Women & Child Development
- Credit up to INR 30 lakh for women

### WE Hub (Telangana)
- India's first state-led incubator for women entrepreneurs
- Website: https://wehub.telangana.gov.in

## Startup Stages Explained

### Ideation Stage
- Problem identified, solution conceptualised
- No product yet, no revenue
- Focus: Validate assumption, customer discovery

### Validation Stage
- Prototype or MVP built
- Some customer validation/feedback
- Focus: Product-market fit

### Early Traction Stage
- Product launched, some paying customers
- Focus: Growth, unit economics

### Scaling Stage
- Proven business model, growing revenue
- Focus: Market expansion, team building

### Established Stage
- Profitable or path to profitability clear
- Focus: Market leadership, international expansion

## Key Performance Metrics for Startups

### SaaS/Subscription Business
- MRR/ARR (Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue)
- Churn Rate (< 5% monthly is good)
- CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
- LTV (Customer Lifetime Value)
- LTV:CAC ratio (> 3x is healthy)

### Marketplace Business
- GMV (Gross Merchandise Value)
- Take Rate
- Buyer/Seller Retention
- NPS (Net Promoter Score)

### Consumer App
- DAU/MAU ratio (engagement)
- Retention D1, D7, D30
- ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)

## B2B vs B2C Startup Considerations

### B2B
- Longer sales cycles (months)
- Higher contract values
- Enterprise sales team needed at scale
- Churn is lower if product is embedded
- Better suited for India because enterprise budgets are growing

### B2C
- Shorter sales cycles
- Lower individual contract values, need high volume
- Marketing-heavy
- Brand building important
- Suited for India's large consumer market
